## Corrections to the previous pilot

- Translations corrected: no. 1 (progener = grandson-in-law of Theodosius the Elder), 4 (Placidus, not Placidius), 7 (ob iudicii sui fidem), 8 (prosocer = grandfather-in-law), 10 (natale is a feast day, not Paul's), 14 (massa pontis restored to the text), 20 and 2 (comes domesticus, not count of the household troops), 26 (patron by descent), 28 (verse syntax), 31 (ranks), 39 (two readings), 42 (niece, cousin; year 379 of the Amasian era = AD 376/7), 43 (Tatianus was assessor to a governor, a vicar, a proconsul and two prefects; these were not his offices).
- Sources recovered: no. 22 from EDR194240 (S. Paolo fuori le mura, Lapidario Paoliano), no. 35 from EDCS-16300170 (AE 1914, 64).
- Working-list slips flagged: no. 2 is LSA-314 (not 344); no. 16 is CIL VI 1674 (not 167); no. 24 is CIL XI 830; no. 29 names Flavius Fortunius, palatinus (not 'Falatinus'); no. 36 reads Antonius Taurus; no. 37 is CIL V 304 = ILCV 1930.
- Places: Trismegistos IDs added for Cingulum (32625) and Sidyma (16439); the Valley of the Kings graffiti now use Memnonia (Pleiades 786067, TM 1341, Thebes west); Rome records placed at LSA/EDR find spots (Forum Romanum, Forum of Trajan, Caelian, Quirinal ×2, Esquiline, S. Paolo, three catacombs).
- Office families revised: quaestors and masters of memorials are court ministers, not finance; centenarius/ducenarius are agentes in rebus grades.

## 01. Flavius Stilicho

*Statue base, Forum Romanum*

- **Reference:** CIL VI 1730 = CIL VI 31913a = ILS 1277
- **Edition used:** [EDCS-18100539](https://edcs.hist.uzh.ch/monument/18100539)
- **Last Statues of Antiquity:** [LSA-1436](http://laststatues.classics.ox.ac.uk/database/detail.php?record=LSA-1436)
- **PLRE:** PLRE I, Stilicho
- **Date:** 398–399
- **Form · language:** Honorific · Latin
- **Office family:** Notaries & tribunes; **offices:** Praetorian tribune (tribunus praetorianus); Count of the household (comes domesticorum / domesticus); Master of the soldiers (magister equitum peditumque)
- **Attribution:** secure
- **Find place:** Roma (Rome, Italy); Pleiades 423025; TM Geo 2058; find spot: Forum Romanum, near SS. Sergius and Bacchus, by the temple of Vespasian (LSA-1436)
- **Now:** Rome, Palazzo Capranica della Valle (LSA)
- **Places named in the text:** Africa — Africa freed (named in the text)
- **Working list:** Honorific inscription of Flavius Stilicho, ?tribunus et notarius praetorianus c. 383; comes sacri stabuli under Theodosius I c. 384, Rome, Forum Romanum, 398-399: CIL VI 1730
- **Image:** [images/inscriptions/i01-stilicho-diptych-cast.jpg](images/inscriptions/i01-stilicho-diptych-cast.jpg) — Related object, not this inscription: Stilicho with Serena and Eucherius, cast of the ivory diptych in Monza (Römisch-Germanisches Zentralmuseum, Mainz). Photo: Bullenwächter, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0 ([source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Stilicho.jpg))

### Text

Fl(avio) Stilichoni v(iro) c(larissimo) / Flavio Stilichoni inlustrissimo viro / magistro equitum peditumque / comiti domesticorum tribuno praetoriano / et ab ineunte aetate per gradus claris/simae militiae ad columen gloriae / sempiternae et regiae adfinitatis evecto / progenero divi Theodosi comiti divi / Theodosi Augusti in omnibus bellis / adque victoriis et ab eo in adfinitatem / regiam cooptato itemque socero d(omini) n(ostri) / Honori Augusti Africa consiliis eius / et provisione liberata ex s(enatus) c(onsulto)

### Provisional translation

To Flavius Stilicho, most distinguished man.

To Flavius Stilicho, most illustrious man, master of cavalry and infantry, count of the household troops, praetorian tribune, raised from his earliest youth through the grades of most distinguished service to the summit of everlasting glory and of kinship with the imperial house; grandson-in-law of the deified Theodosius; companion of the deified Theodosius Augustus in all his wars and victories, and admitted by him into the imperial family; likewise father-in-law of our lord Honorius Augustus. Africa was freed by his counsel and foresight. By decree of the senate.

### Notes

- Progener divi Theodosi: Stilicho's wife Serena was the granddaughter of Theodosius the Elder, so the title means grandson-in-law; the companion in war is Theodosius I.
- The working list's tribunus et notarius praetorianus (c. 383) and comes sacri stabuli (c. 384) are known from other sources; this stone has tribunus praetorianus and comes domesticorum.

## 02. Flavius Eugenius

*Statue base, Forum of Trajan*

- **Reference:** CIL VI 1721 = ILS 1244
- **Edition used:** [EDCS-18100528](https://edcs.hist.uzh.ch/monument/18100528)
- **Last Statues of Antiquity:** [LSA-314](http://laststatues.classics.ox.ac.uk/database/detail.php?record=LSA-314)
- **PLRE:** PLRE I, s.v. Eugenius (mag. off. under Constans)
- **Date:** 355–360
- **Form · language:** Honorific · Latin
- **Office family:** Court offices & household; **offices:** Master of offices (magister officiorum); Count of the household (comes domesticorum / domesticus); Praetorian prefect (praefectus praetorio)
- **Attribution:** secure
- **Find place:** Roma (Rome, Italy); Pleiades 423025; TM Geo 2058; find spot: Forum of Trajan, Forum Traiani (LSA)
- **Now:** Rome, Forum of Trajan, deposit of the Basilica Ulpia (FT 14454)
- **Working list:** Base for posthumously re-erected gilded bronze statue of Flavius Eugenius, master of offices and consul designate; commanded by the emperors. Rome, Forum of Trajan. 355-360: CIL VI 1721=LSA-344
- **Image:** [images/inscriptions/i02-eugenius-cil-vi-1721.jpg](images/inscriptions/i02-eugenius-cil-vi-1721.jpg) — CIL VI 1721 on Géza Alföldy's working sheet, with a photograph of the base. Géza Alföldy, working sheet for CIL VI 8 (drawing), via EAGLE / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0 ([source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Roman_Inscription_from_Roma,_Italy_(CIL_VI_01721).jpeg))

### Text

Fl(avio) Eugenio v(iro) c(larissimo) ex praefecto praetorio / consuli ordinario designato magistro / officiorum omnium comiti domestico / ordinis primi omnibusque palatinis / dignitatibus functo ob egr{a}egia eius / in rem publicam merita huic / dd(omini) nn(ostri) Constantius victor ac / triumfator semper Augustus et / Iulianus nobilissimus Caesar / statuam sub auro in foro divi / Traiani quam ante sub divo / Co(n)stante vitae et fidelissimae / devotionis gratia meruit / adprobante amplissimo senatu / sumptu publico loco suo / restituendam censuerunt

### Provisional translation

To Flavius Eugenius, most distinguished man, former praetorian prefect, consul designate, master of all the offices, first-rank count of the household (comes domesticus), who held every palatine dignity. On account of his outstanding services to the state, our lords Constantius, victor and triumphator, ever Augustus, and Julian, most noble Caesar, decreed that the gilded statue in the Forum of the deified Trajan, which he had earlier earned under the deified Constans for his life and most faithful devotion, be restored to its place at public expense, with the approval of the most ample senate.

### Notes

- The working list gives LSA-344; that LSA record is an epigram for Aurelianus at Constantinople. The base of Eugenius is LSA-314.
- Comes domesticus ordinis primi is a rank in the imperial household, not the command of the household troops (comes domesticorum).

### Text

]es[3] / adque(!) constantia aeq(ue) proba/to v(iro) c(larissimo) Tauro comiti ordinis primi / quaestori sacri palati(i) patricia dig/nitate praef(ecto) praet(orio) per Italiam atq(ue) / Africam dd(omini) nn(ostri) Valentinianus / et Valens victores ac triumfatores / semper Augusti statuam sub auro / quam adprobante amplissimo senatu / iamdudum meruerat ad perpetuam / laudabilis viri memoriam reddi / iusserunt

### Provisional translation

[...] proven alike in [...] and in constancy, to Taurus, most distinguished man, first-rank count, quaestor of the sacred palace, of patrician rank, praetorian prefect for Italy and Africa: our lords Valentinian and Valens, victors and triumphators, ever Augusti, ordered that the gilded statue which, with the approval of the most ample senate, he had long since deserved, be returned to the lasting memory of a praiseworthy man.

### Notes

- The opening lines are lost. Taurus, consul in 361, was condemned at Chalcedon under Julian; the statue was 'returned' under Valentinian and Valens.

### Provisional translation

To Nicomachus Flavianus, governor (consularis) of Sicily, vicar of Africa, quaestor of the palace of the deified Theodosius, praetorian prefect of Italy, Illyricum and Africa for the second time, on account of his senatorial and judicial virtue and authority; [the statue] restored in honour of his son Nicomachus Flavianus, governor of Campania, proconsul of Asia, several times urban prefect, now praetorian prefect of Italy, Illyricum and Africa.

The emperors and Caesars Flavius Theodosius and Flavius Placidus Valentinianus, ever Augusti, greet their senate. To restore, against the accidents of the human condition, the honour of distinguished and illustrious men who served the state, and to call the memory of the dead back into lasting light, seems a kind of correction of the lot that is reckoned the greatest prejudice and injury to virtue. You understand, conscript fathers, that whatever we undertake to restore the former honour of the illustrious Flavianus the Elder, of most sacred memory, is reverence for our deified grandfather. He wished Flavianus to live for us and be preserved for you, as many of you remember from his words; we therefore recall him to the monuments and inscriptions of his virtues, so that you may judge that whatever was done against him by blind accusation was far from the wish of that prince, whose goodwill towards him went so far as to want the annals dedicated to him by his quaestor and prefect, and so provoked the envy of the wicked.

If we have now explained to you the reasons of our piety, accept something more: we are supported by your feelings towards him and by the judgement of all the provinces, in which the goods of a then richer state were preserved or even increased through him, and which brought us such regard for him that we know what we do today has lived in your hearts without any interval of forgetting. For this, conscript fathers, you have above all done no less for his memory than for us, so that we rightly thank your patience, lest we seem to have acted on reminder rather than of our own will in restoring his honour. Besides, his son Flavianus, often approved by us and by our parents for his upbringing, would think his honour half-complete even at the summit of the praetorian prefecture, which his foresight and diligence daily enhance, unless the standing of his whole house and family were at last made whole and free of any debt of duty. Rejoice with us, then, conscript fathers, in this excellent work of our reign; recognise with us that the memory and rank of a senator have been given back to you and to the fatherland. By his company you were the more illustrious, and in his descendants you keep the same regard with us.

I, Appius Nicomachus Dexter, most distinguished man, former urban prefect, saw to the erection [of the statue] for my excellent grandfather.

[Side text:] Dedicated on the [...] of September, in the consulship of Bassus and Antiochus, most distinguished men.

- Front found in 1849, back in 1933 (LSA). The imperial letter is heavily restored in places; the translation follows the EDCS reading and is provisional.
- The emperors are Theodosius II and Valentinian III; the 'deified grandfather' is Theodosius I.
