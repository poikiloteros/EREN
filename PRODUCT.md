# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

Static, single-file HTML page (inline CSS/JS, embedded fonts and data, bundled Leaflet) plus a sibling `images/` folder, regenerated by a small Python build script. Must open offline from the file system; online layers are optional extras only. (Confirmed in the original brief: "produce a single-file html at this stage".)

## Users

Primary: scholars and students of late antique history and epigraphy who come to read, search, filter and cite the inscriptions of fourth-century palatine officials, and to follow the project's work. Secondary: the University of Warsaw / Faculty of History and MSCA evaluators, who need to see the project, its funding, team and outputs clearly.

## Product Purpose

The website of EREN — "Mapping the Central Administration in the Later Roman Empire: The Epigraphic Representation and Elite Networks of the Palatine Bureaucracy", a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Postdoctoral Fellowship (proposal 101152703) held by Dr Mariana Bodnaruk at the Faculty of History, University of Warsaw, with Dr Paweł Nowakowski as advisor. It presents the project (news, conferences, project info, prosopography, gallery, team, funding, contact) and a pilot inscription atlas: an interactive map of 45 inscriptions with full texts, provisional translations, source links, PLRE references and place identifiers. Success: a scholar can find an inscription, read it with its translation and source, see where it sits and which places and offices it names, and trust what is secure versus conjectural.

## Positioning

A research instrument for one specific question — the spatial and social representation and mobility of palatine office-holders — rather than a general epigraphic database. Each record is tied to its edition (EDCS, PHI, EDR) and to unified place notation (Pleiades + Trismegistos Geo), and editorial doubts are shown, not hidden.

## Operating Context

Readers arrive from academic links, the Faculty site, or conference materials; they read Latin and Greek editions with Leiden sigla, compare with EDCS/PHI/LSA/PLRE, and expect to cite records. Desktop is the main working context; phones are used for quick look-ups and the project news.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Sections from the original site must all be present: News, conferences (the "Inscribed Art" CfP / conference), Project info, Prosopography, Gallery, Team, Funding institutions, plus contact.
- Original site text is reused as closely as possible; only light language corrections. No invented aims, slogans or claims.
- Atlas: hover popups with key info; click opens a half-screen reader with full text, translation, PLRE reference; reader can go full screen and back; collapsible side panel (opens on hover) with full-text search and filters (office, form, date, language, certainty); only filtered records appear on the map; office categories coloured; mobility shown only as connections the texts support.
- Translations are provisional and must be labelled as such; uncertain identifications must be flagged.
- Pronouns: Dr Bodnaruk is referred to as they/them throughout (confirmed).

## Brand Commitments

- University of Warsaw / Faculty of History identity (Faculty of History logotype, UW logo) visible in header/footer but not dominating the visual style.
- EU "Funded by the European Union" emblem and MSCA logo shown in a funding section as on the original site.
- Project name EREN; full title shown in the hero.

## Evidence on Hand

- Original site text and images: `../research` snapshots and the live site https://dev-eren.ckc.uw.edu.pl/ (news posts, gallery photos from Aphrodisias, team portraits, logos, CfP PDF).
- Working list of 45 inscriptions (`../EREN inscriptions of palatine officials.docx`, extracted to `../source-list-extracted.md`), with EDCS/PHI/EDR source snapshots.
- Open-licence photographs and archival drawings from Wikimedia Commons (credited per image).
- No testimonials, metrics, publications list or event programme exist yet; none may be invented.

## Product Principles

1. The evidence leads: every claim on a record points to its edition and place identifiers.
2. Show doubt honestly: restorations, discrepancies with the working list and conjectural offices are visible.
3. Mobility only as attested: connections drawn on the map are relationships named in the texts, never reconstructed routes.
4. Faithful to the project's own words: original copy is preserved with minimal correction.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard-operable atlas and reader; reduced-motion respected; sufficient contrast on a light ground; correct `lang` attributes on Latin and Greek texts; inclusive language (they/them for the PI).
